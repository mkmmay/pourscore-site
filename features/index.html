01

Guided pours

Prop your phone up and pour. A path draws itself across the cup in time with the pour, so you always know where the milk should go next. Phases move on by themselves, so your hands stay on the jug.

  • Hands free. Large type, nothing to tap mid-pour.
  • Speed control. Run at full, half or quarter speed while you learn.
  • Your jug and cup. Set up your station once and the guide fits it.

02

Live tilt reading

Your phone knows its own angle. Rest it against the jug or the cup and Pour Score shows the angle live, turning green when you are inside the target for that phase.

  • Real numbers. Degrees, not guesses.
  • Jug height rail. See when to pull away and when to get close.
  • No extra hardware. It uses the sensor already in your phone.

03

Dry rehearsal

Practise the hand movement without wasting milk. A trace scrolls down the screen, drawn from the pattern itself, and you mirror it with an empty jug until the rhythm feels natural.

  • Wiggles and pushes. The same motion data as the real pour.
  • Zero cost. Rehearse in the evening, pour in the morning.
  • Straight into the real thing. One tap from rehearsal to a guided pour.

04

Milk first

Great art starts with great milk. A short tutorial covers stretching, texture and swirling, and a pattern can wait until you have it. Read the chapters in order, once, then use them as a quick reference.

  • Plain chapters. What to do, what it should look like, what went wrong.
  • Fixes for common faults. Beige milk, big bubbles, thin foam.

Progress and streaks

Log each pour with a photo and a score for how it felt. Your streak, your history and your tier live in one place, and they stay private unless you decide to share a pour.

  • Streaks. A flame that lights when you pour and a personal best to beat.
  • Five tiers. Unlocked from real pours, never self-declared.
  • Public or private. You choose, pour by pour.
