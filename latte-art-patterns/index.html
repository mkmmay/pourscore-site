Latte art patterns, from monk's head to swan

Nine coffee art patterns, grouped by the tier Pour Score teaches them in. Each one has the steps and the fix for its most common fault.

The monk's head is a heart without the tail, and every other pattern is built on it. Add a pull-through and you have a heart. Add a wiggle and you have a rosetta. Push dollops into each other and you have a tulip. Learn them in that order and each new pattern adds only one new movement.

Jump to: Monk's Head · Heart · Tulip · Rippled Heart · Rosetta · Stacked Tulip · Swan · Etched Animals · Stacked Rosetta

Start every pattern with the base

Swirl the crema away first, tilt the cup a little and start low in the middle. Rise 2 to 3 cm and move gently side to side until the cup is as full as the pattern needs, a figure given in each section below. Stay off the edges, or foam rides up the wall and shows white.

Beginner

How to pour a latte art monk's head

A heart without the tail. One drop, no wiggle, the shape every other pattern is built on.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 30% first.
  2. One drop, speed up. Drop in near the back of the tilted cup and pour faster and faster.

If it goes wrong. Shape stuck in the middle of the cup means you poured too gently. Speed up as it fills and let the milk push itself out to the wall.

Home Pourer

How to pour a latte art heart

A monk's head with a tail pulled through it. One drop in, flow rate climbing, then up and through.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 45% first.
  2. Drop in close, speed up. Tilt the cup, spout almost touching, and drop in about a centimetre in from the back. Stay in that one spot and pour faster as it fills.
  3. Lift and pull through. Slow down just before it is full, lift high and draw one fast line through the middle.

If it goes wrong. Fat line through the middle means you stayed too close for the pull-through. Go back up high and draw faster, speed makes the line thin and straight.

How to pour a latte art tulip

Dollops pushed forward into each other: three layers and a small heart on top, then one quick pull-through.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 35% first.
  2. First dollop, then push behind. Drop in near the centre, push forward and stop. Drop the next one just behind it and push it into the first. Three layers, then a small heart on top.
  3. Up and through. Lift high and draw one fast line down through every dollop.

If it goes wrong. Blobs merging into one lump means you left no brown gap between them. Stop fully, push the last dollop forward, and start the next one behind it.

Confident

How to pour a latte art rippled heart

One heart, layered from the inside. Drop in, keep the spout on one spot and rock gently so the milk ripples out in rings, fill the centre, then pull through.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 50% first.
  2. Drop in, then ripple. Drop in close, just past the centre. Once the white shows, rock the jug gently side to side, keeping the spout close and almost still.
  3. Up and through. When the cup is almost level, lift a little and draw one line through the heart.

If it goes wrong. Rings smeared into one blob? The jug is travelling. Keep the spout on one spot and let only the milk move. Lopsided rings? Rock the same small amount each way.

How to pour a latte art rosetta

The heart plus a wiggle. Small, slow, even rocking while the milk does the spreading for you.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 60% first.
  2. Drop in the middle, then wiggle. Stay high over the base, then come down close in the middle of the cup and start rocking straight away. Keep the jug in the middle while the leaves push away from you, then zigzag back to the near rim.
  3. Up and through. At the near rim, hold a beat for the small heart, then lift high and draw one line back through every leaf.

If it goes wrong. Leaves thin and cramped? Your pour slowed down while you concentrated on rocking, that is the fight in this pattern. Fat stem? You stayed close for the pull-through instead of lifting high.

Café Ready

How to pour a latte art stacked tulip

Six or seven tulip layers, each dropped a little closer to you and pushed into the last, then one pull-through. A drill in clean stops.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 45% first.
  2. Build the stack. Drop in at the centre, push forward, stop. Start each next layer a little closer to you and push it into the last.
  3. Up and through. Lift a little and pull one quick line from the small heart through every layer.

If it goes wrong. Stacks drifting off-axis? You are pouring from the wrist. Move the whole jug.

Barista

How to pour a latte art swan

A rosetta wing you do not pull through, a slow neck drawn close down the side of it, then a small heart for the head.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 60% first.
  2. Drop in, then wing. Drop in close off to one side, then rock like a rosetta, forward and back, without pulling through.
  3. Body and neck. Spout right on the surface, draw slowly down the side of the wing towards you, then hook out to the side.
  4. Head and beak. Stay where the neck ends and let a little more foam out for the head, then lift and cut up through it, slightly to the side, for the beak.

If it goes wrong. Neck too thin or sunk into the wing? You drew it too fast or too early. Wait until the cup is nearly full, get the spout right on the surface and slow right down.

Etched Animals

Bear, kitten, seahorse. Free-poured bases finished with a needle or thermometer tip.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 35% first.
  2. Pour the base shapes. A big dot for the head, two small dots for the ears.

If it goes wrong. Etch within about twenty seconds of the pour. After that the foam sets and the lines go ragged.

How to pour a latte art stacked rosetta

The advanced rosetta. A push base and heavy ripples in the middle, a small push forward, then a fast pull back so the leaves come out wide and chunky, finished with a heavier heart and a gentle cut.

  1. Lay the base. Fill the cup to about 40% first.
  2. Push base, then ripple in place. Land close, just above the middle. As the spout touches, guide the jug forward and back to the middle without breaking the stream, then ripple heavily side to side on the spot.
  3. Pull back fast, rippling wide. Give one more heavy rock, then pull straight back to the near rim while rippling wide.
  4. Heavy heart, gentle cut. At the near rim, hold still (or give one last push) for a heavier top heart, then lift a little and cut through gently.

If it goes wrong. Leaves thin and crowded? You pulled back too slowly. Pull back about twice as fast as a normal rosetta and ripple wider. Colour washed out of the leaves at the end? You cut too heavily, keep the pull-through light.
