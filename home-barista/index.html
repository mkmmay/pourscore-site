Home barista guide: milk, jugs and your first latte art

What a home barista needs before the first pour: how much milk each drink takes, which jug and cup to use, and how to steam the milk. The sizes and steps are the ones Pour Score's coach uses.

How much milk for each drink

Starting points for each drink. Adjust to your own cup.

Which milk jug

JugBest for
350 ml jug12 oz. For piccolos, cortados and a single flat white.
450 ml jug15 oz. The all-rounder, one latte or two smaller drinks.

Which cup

The cup sets how much room you have for a pattern.

CupSizeWhat it means for your pour
Espresso cup4 oz, about 120 mlSmallest and shallowest. Barely any surface, one drop, done.
Flat white cup6 oz, about 175 mlWide and shallow. Slow wrap, the jug does more of the work.
Cappuccino cup7 oz, about 205 mlYour default. Rounded, forgiving, good surface area.
Latte glass8 oz, about 235 mlTaller than a cappuccino cup with a similar surface. Room for a bigger pattern.
Tall mug14 oz, about 415 mlDeep and narrow. Surface shrinks fast, start the pattern late, keep it simple.

Steaming milk in two steps

  1. Aeration, make the bubbles. Drop the pitcher so the tip breaks the surface. It sounds like an aggressive kiss, or a straw in an almost-empty glass. Aim for bubbles too small to see, in two to four bursts, and get it all done early.
  2. Incorporation, split them down. Lift slightly so the tip sits just under the surface without pulling in new air, and let the whirlpool do the work. The milk rises as the bubbles expand, so watch the level and cut it before it climbs out.

Height and sound beat timers. Time and temperature both punish you for aerating at an uneven rate. Height is the most repeatable on one machine; sound travels best between machines, the pitch drops from kissing to slurping as air goes in.

Good milk behaves like a liquid. If the pour feels like you are pushing a foamy substance around, the texture is wrong, no amount of wrist will save the pattern. Thicker milk needs a wider wiggle and more flow; thinner milk needs gentler, steadier movement.
