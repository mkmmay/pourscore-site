**You can practise latte art without wasting milk by rehearsing the movement with an empty jug, drilling the wiggle on its own, or pouring something that is not milk.**

Every pour costs milk, and most early pours end up down the sink. After a week of that, it's hard not to feel a bit wasteful.

Here's the thing, though. Most of what goes wrong isn't the milk. It's your hand. The height, the speed, the moment you stop. And your hand can learn all of that without any milk at all.

Here are three ways to practise. Start with the first one.

## 1. Rehearse the movement with an empty jug

Grab an empty jug and an empty cup. Tilt the cup, bring the spout close, then lift and draw through, the way you would for a heart. The pull-through is about a second and a half, high and fast, so do it that fast.

It looks daft in the kitchen. It works anyway.

Pour Score's dry rehearsal does the same thing with a trace scrolling down the screen. You copy it with the empty jug, step by step, and nothing gets poured. It doesn't count towards your tiers, which is the point: no pressure, no milk.

Rehearse in the evening, pour in the morning.

## 2. Drill the wiggle on its own

The wiggle is where most people come unstuck. In a rosetta you rock for about three seconds in the middle, then zigzag back to the near rim over about two. Small, slow, even.

You can practise that rhythm by itself. In the app, the wiggle rehearsal loops just the rocking, with no pouring and no tilt, purely to get your wrist moving. It's there for the patterns that have a wiggle. The heart and the monk's head don't, because they're a single controlled drop.

Stop when your wrist gets tired, not when you get bored.

## 3. Pour something that isn't milk

Some trainers practise with water, a drop of dish soap and a little old espresso, so the jug still has weight and the stream still behaves a bit like a pour. La Marzocco's home team describes this as a way to practise on repeat without using up milk. Barista Carl's Blend sells drops for the same idea, and the maker says they're for training only.

Two things to know. It's not milk, so it won't foam or behave exactly like it, and it's undrinkable. Don't taste it, and keep it away from anything you'd drink. I haven't got a tested recipe to give you, so I won't pretend to quote amounts. Try it small, and compare each go with the last one.

## What I'd do

Keep it simple. One pour a day, and a few minutes of dry rehearsal on the days you don't pour. If you only try one thing from this list, make it the first.
