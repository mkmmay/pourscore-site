**Look at the milk before you pour: if the surface is dull with big bubbles, you added too much air, and if it's glossy but runs like skimmed water, you added too little.**

You've steamed a jug and it looks fine. Then the pour goes wrong anyway. Washed-out shape, blobs, loose swirls that won't hold. Most of the time the milk was telling you something thirty seconds earlier, and nobody taught you how to read it.

Here's what to look for, and what to change.

## Look at the surface first

Give the jug a swirl and tilt it. You want a glossy surface, a bit like wet paint. No dome of foam on top, no visible bubbles.

If it isn't like that, it's one of two things.

## Bubbly: too much air

**What you see:** large bubbles, or dry, dull foam sitting on top. When you pour, the pattern comes out washed out or blobby, with no crisp lines.

**Why:** the milk had too much air in it, or it sat too long before you poured.

**What to change:** stretch the milk for less time. Add less air while you steam, and swirl the jug until it looks like wet paint. If you have a dome of foam sitting on top, keep swirling until it's gone, then pour straight away. A jug that has been left standing separates, and the foam floats up as one blob.

## Thin: too little air

**What you see:** the milk looks smooth, but the pattern spreads into loose swirls and won't hold its shape.

**Why:** thin milk with too little air in it.

**What to change:** stretch the milk a little longer next time, until it looks like wet paint. Small steps. A few more seconds of air is plenty to see the difference.

There's a second thing to watch with thin milk. If you pour hard into it, the milk runs underneath and hits the back wall of the cup, and the base breaks up. Lay the jug back gently into the cup before you push forward.

## Change your pour to suit the milk

Sometimes you won't get the milk perfect, and that's fine. You can still adjust.

- **Thicker milk** needs a wider wiggle and more flow.
- **Thinner milk** needs gentler, steadier movement.

Change one thing at a time. If you change the milk and the pour together, you won't know which one fixed it.

## A quick check, every jug

1. Swirl the jug.
2. Look for a dome or visible bubbles. If you see them, keep swirling.
3. Check the shine. Glossy is good, dull is too much air.
4. Check how it moves. It should flow like a liquid, not sit there like foam.
5. Pour straight away.
